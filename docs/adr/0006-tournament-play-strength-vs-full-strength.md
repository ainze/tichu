# ADR-0006: Tournament has a play-strength variant (no Schupfen) and a full-strength variant (with Schupfen)

- **Status:** Superseded by [ADR-0025](0025-full-strength-tournament-is-the-only-variant-and-includes-calls.md) — the Play-strength variant is retired and Full-strength now exercises the complete stack including both Call Networks.
- **Date:** 2026-05-25
- **Related:** [ADR-0003](0003-easy-difficulty-is-a-baseline.md), [ADR-0025](0025-full-strength-tournament-is-the-only-variant-and-includes-calls.md)

## Context

The Tournament harness measures relative Agent strength over a fixed
Starting-Position Pool. The current Pool is built by `deal_initial_state`,
which **skips Schupfen** — cards are dealt and the current player is set to
the Mahjong holder directly. As `tichu_eval/deal_pool.py:5-6` puts it:

> Schupfen is intentionally skipped — the harness measures play strength,
> not schupfen heuristics.

This is a deliberate isolation: Schupfen is a tactically meaningful but
small surface (one Decision per Round, 3 cards), and folding it into
play-strength evaluation would conflate two things that improve
independently. A weak Schupfen Head paired with a strong Play Head would
look like a worse Agent than it really is at the post-Schupfen phase.

However, the final shipping product plays Schupfen too. **A Checkpoint
that wins every play-strength Tournament can still ship a weak
post-Schupfen position to every Round and lose at the table.** Play
strength is necessary but not sufficient for product quality.

## Decision

**Maintain two Tournament variants:**

1. **Play-strength Tournament.** Starting-Position Pool generated by
   `deal_initial_state(seed + i)`. Schupfen skipped. Measures the Play /
   Wish / Dragon-Assignment / Tichu-Call axes in isolation. This is the
   current Tournament — keep it as the primary regression metric for the
   Policy Network and Call Networks.

2. **Full-strength Tournament.** Starting-Position Pool generated by
   `deal_for_schupfen(seed + i)` (already exists in
   `tichu_engine/state.py`). Schupfen is played by each Agent's own
   Schupfen Head. Measures end-to-end product strength. Required for any
   Checkpoint that ships to a Difficulty tier.

Every shipped Checkpoint produces a row in **both** matrices.

## Rationale

1. **Schupfen and Play are independent training surfaces.** They share the
   Trunk but optimise against different label distributions. Confounding
   them in eval means a Schupfen regression looks like a Play regression
   and vice versa — debugging that confusion costs more than running two
   Tournaments.
2. **Play-strength matters per Trick; Schupfen matters once per Round.**
   The variance contribution of Schupfen to final Round score is smaller
   than play, but its *bias* is not — a systematic bad-card-to-opponent
   pattern costs a few points every Round. Bias requires its own metric.
3. **Two matrices, one Pool generator difference.** Implementation cost is
   small: the Starting-Position Pool already has a `deal_for_schupfen`
   constructor. The Tournament code itself is unchanged — Schupfen is just
   the first few Decisions instead of being pre-resolved.
4. **The Difficulty Spec only ships from Full-strength results.** Master
   beating Hard in Play-strength but losing in Full-strength means the
   Master Refined Checkpoint has a Schupfen regression — promote nothing
   on Play-strength alone.

## Consequences

- The Tournament Matrix becomes two artefacts per eval run:
  `play_strength_matrix.parquet` and `full_strength_matrix.parquet`.
- Move Prediction Eval is unchanged — it already covers Schupfen
  decisions (Schupfen is a Decision type with its own parquet shard).
- The Starting-Position Pool format must distinguish its two
  variants — recommend `play_pool_*.parquet` vs `full_pool_*.parquet`
  with the same `(seed, n)` identity scheme.
- Existing CI / eval CLIs that produce only the Play-strength matrix
  must be extended to also produce Full-strength before any Checkpoint
  is promoted to a Difficulty slot.
- A Checkpoint that regresses on Full-strength but holds Play-strength
  is a real diagnostic signal — most likely a Schupfen Head regression.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Single Tournament including Schupfen.** Conflates two axes; makes
  Play-only regression invisible.
- **Single Tournament excluding Schupfen, with a separate offline Schupfen
  benchmark.** Doesn't measure the *interaction* between Schupfen and
  downstream Play (e.g. an Agent that passes its strongest cards to the
  opponent then plays well — Play-strength + offline-Schupfen would not
  catch this; Full-strength does).
